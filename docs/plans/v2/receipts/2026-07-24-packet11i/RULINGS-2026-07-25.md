## 1. THE ONLY THREE WORTH YOUR ATTENTION — [LEAD CALL], veto cheaply

These three do NOT follow from a stated directive. Each is a judgement I made so the packet can move;
each is cheap to reverse now and expensive later, so if any looks wrong, say the number.

**L1 — decision 2, the ≥98% gate's sample population: RULED R-PACKAGE** (answered union + identifier +
absent leg, anchored excluded). The two readings differ by 4.1 points against a 2% budget, so this is
load-bearing.

**Operator response 2026-07-25: *"I have no idea. You'll need to give me more details."*** — so the
detail is recorded HERE rather than in a chat message, and the ruling below is the lead's, taken after
supplying it.

**What the gate does, plainly.** The floor is measured with uncertainty, so it arrives as an interval,
not a point. The gate asks whether that interval is tight enough that the floor's exact value does not
change what an agent SEES: it computes every test query's verdict twice — once at `ci_low`, once at
`ci_high` — and counts how many differ. ≤2% differing ⇒ tight enough ⇒ adopt that N.

**The choice is which queries are counted.** Three kinds exist: **real** (the answer IS in the corpus —
the floor must not fire), **absent/hold-out** (it genuinely is not — the floor SHOULD fire), and
**anchored** (the query carries a verbatim identifier — the predicate never fires, at any floor).

**Sub-decision (a) — anchored samples: EXCLUDE. This is not a judgement call.** **[source-verified]**
`_cosine_absence_predicate` returns False on any anchored sample regardless of floor, so anchored
samples CANNOT flip. Including them adds a block of guaranteed agreements to the denominator: a corpus
that is 30% anchored yields 30% agreement for free, and the gate passes on arithmetic rather than on
stability. That is the non-discriminating-fixture class this repo has been bitten by four times, wearing
a gate's clothing.

**Sub-decision (b) — the absent leg: INCLUDE. This is the real trade, and both costs are real.**
- *Including it* measures whether the floor is stable at the thing the feature EXISTS to do — firing on
  absent queries. Cost, stated honestly: absent-leg scores cluster near the floor by construction, so
  more of them flip, the gate is harder to pass, adopted N rises, and measurement costs more.
- *Excluding it* is cheaper and matches D2's literal words. Cost: catch behaviour can wobble across the
  interval while the gate reports STABLE — a floor steady at not-false-firing and shaky at actually
  catching, with nothing to say so.

**Why the lead ruled rather than deferring again:** written out, it derives from stated directives — P2
(*do it right the first time*) rules out certifying half the contract, and the Trust Doctrine rules out
a silent wobble in precisely the surface agents are told to trust.

**Re-open trigger (the honest one):** R2's real data. If the absent leg turns out small, or its scores
sit far from the floor, the extra cost buys nothing and R-literal is fine. That measurement is coming,
so this is settled for now rather than settled forever.

**L2 — lease tunables: UPSTREAM DEFAULTS (15s / 10s / 2s), not the sidecar's 60/20/5.**
✅ **OPERATOR-CONFIRMED 2026-07-25: *"use the defaults"*.** This overrides the design sidecar, and the
override is now the operator's, not the lead's. Reasoning: P1's *rationale* is that a package is
better tested — and its defaults are part of what is tested. `client-go`'s values are the ones running
at scale everywhere. A divergence should be earned by a measurement, and none exists. **Re-open
trigger:** a measured renewal-failure rate under real store latency.

**L3 — decision 21, BCa: NOT ADOPTED** (kept as a named contingency).
✅ **OPERATOR-CONFIRMED 2026-07-25: *"if you say so"*** — accepted on the lead's reasoning, so the
reasoning had better be recorded: adopting BCa would amend D8's pre-registered "central 90%
percentile", a statistical change with no evidence demanding it, and pre-registration exists precisely

independently judged, it carries a **lower confidence flag**: if the percentile interval later fails a
coverage check on R2's real distribution, re-open it without deference to this ruling. **Re-open
trigger:** exactly that coverage check.

## 4. The process correction, recorded so it does not recur

The failure was not that the lead escalated too much in general — scope law is explicit that nothing
is deferred or dismissed without the operator, and that stands. **The failure was escalating
INSTANCES of a rule the operator had already given.** Once *"add any package that does the job"* is
stated, "should we adopt sklearn?" and "should we take the kubernetes dependency?" are not questions;
they are the rule being applied, and returning them for signature converts a decision into a queue.

**The test to apply before escalating, going forward:** *can this be derived from something the
operator has already said?* If yes, derive it, record the derivation, and move — the operator can veto
a recorded ruling far more cheaply than they can answer an open question. If no, it is a genuine fork
and it escalates, with a recommendation.

That distinction is what §1 and §2 of this document are: three genuine calls, twenty derivations.
